A small machine, opened up

Turn the model with your mouse. Run the transport, open the cassette compartment, or move the covers aside to see how the mechanism fits together.

This model is an estimate. It was worked out from photographs, videos, Sony’s service manual and the Compact Cassette standard, and no part has yet been checked against a real unit. Every part carries a confirmation level, from 1 (modelled, not checked) to 6 (measured on two units by two people); today all are at level 1.

Serial number 12345 was chosen completely arbitrarily for this model and is not taken from any real unit.

What the levels mean
    Take part

    Three ways, each fine by itself.

    1. Enjoy it and share it. It is free to use and share with credit (MIT licence).
    2. Send a measurement. Pick the part in Inspect and choose “Confirm a feature”. Describe the measured feature and how you measured it; the maintainer reviews the evidence.
    3. Adjust a part yourself. Edit your own copy in Blender and send a link to it with the measurement it rests on.

    About this model

    Explore the Sony TPS-L2 through its component IDs, model dimensions and operating controls. Belts use fixed paths with traveling motion cues. Audio consists of locally generated test signals.

    How to present

    Start the guided tour for a short walkthrough, or use the transport and view controls. Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, and right-drag or two-finger drag to pan. In Operate, click the modeled buttons, drag volume grips or the Tone switch, and hold Hot Line. In Inspect, click a component, then Focus or Isolate.

    Space pauses motion. 0 resets. Escape closes component details and returns focus to their opener; a select keeps Escape for its native menu. Use Collapse details on narrow screens to keep the component name visible while hiding the detail controls. This page works the same opened from disk or from a web host. Arrow keys rotate the focused model; + and − zoom.

    Release & performance

    Portable Tape Player v0.3.1. Physical accuracy is limited: no part has been checked against a real unit yet. The README says how to help confirm one.
